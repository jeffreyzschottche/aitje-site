# What is a context window?

Official page: https://aitje.com/en/knowledge/wat-is-een-context-window

> The context window is the maximum amount of information a model can process in a single interaction. This determines how much text, chat history or document fragments can be used at any one time.

### In plain language

You can think of a context window as a model’s working memory. The larger that working memory, the more text, previous messages and documents the model can take into account simultaneously to formulate a response.

### Why is this important?

With long documents, extensive chats and larger knowledge bases, you can quickly reach your limits. In such cases, not all relevant information can be included in one go. The system must therefore decide what to include and what to leave out.

### What happens when you hover over it?

If you try to provide more information than fits within the context window, the system will have to shorten, summarise or omit parts of the text. As a result, important nuances may be lost. In some cases, the model may then appear careless, whilst in fact it has simply been given too little useful context.

### Bigger isn’t always better

A larger context window is useful, but no guarantee of quality. If you add poor or irrelevant information, the answer will not automatically improve. Relevant selection, a good summary and clear instructions remain more important than simply having more tokens available.

### Practical implications

In real-world applications, the context window determines how you structure documents, how long a chat history remains usable and how you organise RAG or summaries. It is therefore not an abstract model detail, but a design choice with a direct impact on usability and reliability.
