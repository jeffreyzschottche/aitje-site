# What is loss-leader pricing in the context of AI?

Official page: https://aitje.com/en/knowledge/wat-is-loss-leader-pricing

> With loss-leader pricing, an offer is priced at a loss to attract customers. How does this differ from a temporary discount, finance options and predatory pricing?

### In brief

Loss-leader pricing is a pricing strategy whereby a product or service is offered below cost price to attract customers. The provider expects, for example, to recoup the investment through other purchases, add-ons or a longer-term customer relationship. You may also hear the term ‘loss-leader price’. This does not mean that every discount or free trial is automatically part of such a strategy.

### What might this look like in the context of AI?

Consider, as a possible scenario, low-cost API usage within a platform that also offers paid storage, management or larger packages. The price of a single component does not, therefore, tell the whole story about the total bill. To determine whether an offer is genuinely loss-making, you need insight into the relevant costs. The visible token price alone is insufficient for this.

### A fictional example

A hypothetical supplier offers a basic function temporarily below cost and expects to generate revenue from a paid management package. A user with only the basic function benefits initially. An organisation purchasing the full package must compare the total cost. This is an illustrative example of the mechanism, not a description of a specific provider or a prediction of price increases.

### The difference with VC subsidy

VC subsidy describes how capital can temporarily finance growth or losses. Loss-leader pricing describes the pricing strategy for an offer. These terms may occur together, but do not necessarily have to. For example, a larger, profitable company can afford a loss-making entry-level product from other sources of income without the involvement of venture capital.

### The difference from predatory pricing

Predatory pricing refers to an exclusionary pricing practice that undermines competition. A low or loss-making price alone does not prove this. For a legal assessment within the EU, factors such as market power, costs and the effect on competition are relevant. Loss-leader pricing should therefore not automatically be labelled as predatory pricing or unlawful conduct.

### What does this mean for your choice of AI?

Compare the entire workflow: model calls, storage, integrations, management and the effort involved in switching providers later on. Check whether trial prices and standard rates differ. A favourable introductory offer can be an excellent choice as long as you understand the terms and conditions. Keep your data and model links transferable wherever possible, so that you still have options even if your needs change.
